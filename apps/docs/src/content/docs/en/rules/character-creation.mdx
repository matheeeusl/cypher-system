## The Character Sentence

To create your character, you make a simple statement that describes them. The character sentence takes this form:

> I am a [descriptor] [type] who [focus].

For example: I am a <EntityLink kind="descriptor" id="mad" />{" "}<EntityLink kind="type" id="explorer" /> who <EntityLink kind="focus" id="walks-the-wild-woods" />.

## Descriptor

The adjective is your descriptor. A descriptor defines what you are like, including some strengths and weaknesses. In some settings, it might also describe your species or other unique things about your personal history. The GM might limit which descriptors are appropriate for the game setting. There are many descriptors, so it's unlikely two PCs in a group will need to use the same one.

## Type

The noun is your character type: <EntityLink kind="type" id="warrior" />, <EntityLink kind="type" id="adept" />, <EntityLink kind="type" id="explorer" />, or <EntityLink kind="type" id="speaker" />. Your character type defines your role in the world, and includes a broad choice of special abilities you might want your character to have. Types are very broad, so it's fine if two PCs have the same type—no two will be alike.

## Focus

The verb is your focus. Focus is what your character does best, granting you new special abilities each tier. For example, <EntityLink kind="focus" id="walks-the-wild-woods" /> grants <EntityLink kind="ability" id="wilderness-life" /> at tier 1. Your focus also helps you understand how you relate with the other player characters in your group. The foci you choose from will probably depend on the setting and genre of your game.

Foci are unique. No two PCs in the group will have the same focus.

## Making Choices

Your descriptor and focus are less flexible than your type, so try narrowing those options down first. Then determine which type best fills in the gaps for your concept.

## Choosing a Connection

Roll a d100 or choose a connection that goes well with your focus. Your choice affects your relationship to one of the other PCs. A few foci also list connection options of their own on their pages.

## Flavor

A flavor is an optional customization of your type. It lets you choose special abilities that are otherwise unavailable to you: at each tier you can take abilities from your flavor's list and permanently exchange them for abilities on your type's list. You still choose the same number of abilities — only the pool you choose from grows. Once you exchange a type ability away, you can't choose it later.

For example, a "shinobi" is a Warrior with the stealth flavor. If they exchange Bash for Danger Sense, they can't choose Bash later.

The GM might instead decide that flavors simply add to your choices for type abilities. Either way, the GM approves each flavor ability, making sure it is of an appropriate tier, fits the game's scope and setting, and won't make another PC's focus or abilities feel redundant.

You might gain a flavor by completing a character arc such as Join an Organization, Master a Skill, Mysterious Background, or Transformation, or by spending 4 XP as a long-term benefit. A flavor can also be named in the character sentence: an Explorer with technology flavor might be the crew's Tongue-Tied (but trusty) "Engineer" who Crafts Unique Objects.
